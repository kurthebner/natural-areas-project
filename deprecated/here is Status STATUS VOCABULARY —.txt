Here is Coordination.
COORDINATION FIELD — v4.0 (Authoritative) Coordination expresses entities that play an official, documented supporting role in the operation, stewardship, or administration of a Site — without being the owner or the manager. This field captures:
- stewardship partners
- volunteer groups
- advisory bodies
- cooperating agencies
- research partners
- maintenance partners
- co‑signatories on agreements
- entities with partial but not primary operational responsibility It is a free‑text field, but governed by strict rules and controlled patterns.
🌿 1. What Coordination is A Coordination entry is appropriate when an entity:
- assists with stewardship
- provides volunteer labor
- provides ecological management
- co‑signs a management agreement
- provides funding for operations
- provides technical expertise
- maintains trails or facilities under agreement
- partners on conservation or restoration
- provides educational programming
- holds a research permit or research stewardship role In short: Coordination = official, documented partnership that is neither ownership nor management.
🌿 2. What Coordination is not Coordination must not be used for:
- ownership
- management
- designation
- access rules
- temporary volunteer events
- informal community use
- one‑off grants
- trail maintenance by random volunteers
- stewardship philosophy
- “friends of” groups unless formally recognized

Location
Purpose
A concise, human‑readable geographic reference that situates the Site in the landscape.
Provides contextual placement without requiring an address or coordinates.
Requirements
- Must be a short phrase or single sentence.
- Must be human‑readable and non‑technical.
- Must not include county names (County is its own field).
- Must not include identity-bearing terms (e.g., “park,” “preserve,” “cemetery”).
- Must not include ownership, management, designation, or access rules.
- Must not contain full street addresses or GPS coordinates.
- Must not contain driving directions.
Allowed Content
- Nearest road(s) or intersection(s).
- Nearest municipality or township.
- Nearby geographic features (creek, river, ridge, valley).
- Placement within a larger complex (e.g., “inside Alum Creek State Park”).
- Directional cues (“north of…”, “east of…”).
- Landmarks used purely for orientation.
Formatting Rules
- Keep concise: one phrase or sentence.
- Use commas sparingly; avoid long chains.
- If referencing multiple municipalities/townships, semicolon‑delimit.
- Do not include county names.
- Do not include narrative identity (that belongs in Description).
Examples
- “Along County Road 45, north of Edison.”
- “Inside Highbanks Metro Park, near the Big Meadows area.”
- “On the west bank of Alum Creek, south of Cheshire Road.”
- “Near the junction of Township Road 180 and County Road 15.”
- “In downtown Galion, between Harding Way and Church Street.”
