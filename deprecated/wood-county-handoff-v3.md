## KEY INSIGHTS

### Governance Patterns in Wood County

1. **Three-Tier Park System:**
   - **State Level (Tier 2):** ODNR manages wildlife areas and one state park
   - **District Level (Tier 3):** Wood County Park District manages major county-wide parks (21 properties, 1,475.5 acres claimed)
   - **Municipal Level (Tier 6):** Cities and villages manage local parks within their boundaries

2. **Townships Do Not Manage Parks:**
   - All 19 townships rely on Wood County Park District
   - No township-owned or township-managed parks found
   - Pattern likely reflects efficiency: one district serves entire county rather than 19 separate township systems

3. **Municipalities Manage Their Own Parks:**
   - All 5 cities have municipal park systems
   - All 4 villages sampled have municipal parks
   - Municipal parks supplement (not replace) county park district facilities
   - Residents have access to both municipal AND district parks

4. **County Government Has No Direct Park Management:**
   - Wood County government does not manage parks
   - All county-level recreation delegated to independent Wood County Park District (established 1934)
   - Some co-management exists (e.g., Adam Phillips Pond)

5. **Wood County Park District is Primary Landholder:**
   - Established 1934 as independent entity
   - 21 properties across 1,475.5 acres (claimed)
   - Strong conservation focus: wetland restoration, native plant propagation, prescribed burns
   - Extensive public access: fishing (7 parks), trails (14 parks), hunting (2 parks by permit)
   - Regional trail connectivity through Maumee and Portage River water trails
